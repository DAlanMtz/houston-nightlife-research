# Houston Nightlife, 2013–2025

[Read the publication](https://DAlanMtz.github.io/houston-nightlife-research/) ·
[Explore the interactive charts](https://DAlanMtz.github.io/houston-nightlife-research/interactive/) ·
[Download the PDF](https://DAlanMtz.github.io/houston-nightlife-research/report/houston-nightlife-full-report.pdf)

## Research question

How did Houston's nightlife filing population grow, thin, relocate, and turn
over from 2013 through 2025, and what can administrative records reliably say
about those changes?

The publication combines Texas mixed-beverage tax receipts, TABC licensing
records, current City of Houston district boundaries, Census data, and BLS
inflation measures. It covers Houston's strict-city nightlife filing population
and a broader lifecycle panel. Reported mixed-beverage taxable receipts are a
tax base—not total business revenue, profit, attendance, or market power.

## What the publication includes

- An eleven-chapter reading edition with methods, limitations, and source notes.
- Interactive charts for growth, concentration, geography, closure states, and
  prediction, each with an accessible data table and no-JavaScript fallback.
- A downloadable 37-page PDF.
- A documented disclosure policy that suppresses small or dominated
  district-year cells and distinguishes suppression from zero and missing data.

## Major findings

- Annual ever-active venues rose from 576 in 2013 to 805 in 2025.
- Real reported receipts rose more slowly than venue count, leaving a smaller
  typical venue by this measure.
- Receipt concentration increased modestly; this is not evidence of market
  power.
- A venue's disappearance from administrative records is not automatically a
  verified closure. The analysis separates missing reports, zero reporting,
  relocation, succession, and unresolved states.
- Replacement concepts often reported more than predecessors in matched
  comparisons, but the result is associative rather than causal.
- A complex warning model did not outperform a simpler deterioration baseline
  on the final future test.

## Limitations

The administrative record does not directly observe closure, ownership intent,
attendance, cultural importance, or profitability. District boundaries are a
current-vintage classification. Geographic coverage varies by year. Several
derived real-receipt measures are explicitly labelled noncanonical, and no
observational result is presented as causal. Small and dominated geographic
cells are suppressed; named venue-level receipts are not published.
