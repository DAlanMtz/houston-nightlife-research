More venues, a smaller typical venue.

Houston's strict-city nightlife filing population grew from 576 annual ever-active venues in 2013 to 805 in 2025. Combined real reported receipts rose 17%, from $442.9M to $519.1M. The count rose faster, 40%, so receipts per venue fell on average by 16%. The median fell further, 26%, from $486,931 to $361,409.

That is the central puzzle. The report does not claim that new venues caused the decline. It shows what the tax record can describe, where the map and the closures become uncertain, and why several promising statistical relationships could not be established.

The full report runs through eleven chapters, from the measurement problem to the limits of prediction. The interactive edition covers the charts in Chapters 3, 4 and 6.

What the numbers are Reported mixed-beverage taxable receipts: liquor, wine, beer, and cover charges. Not revenue, profit, attendance, or market power.

Reading time About five minutes for the short version in the report; about an hour for the full evidence trail.
